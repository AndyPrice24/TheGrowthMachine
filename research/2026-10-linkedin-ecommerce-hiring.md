# Why consumer and ecommerce brands hire growth leadership: LinkedIn job-ad research (round two)

Read on 9 October 2026. Filter: Date posted = Past month. Read-only. "Posted" ages are relative to that date.

## Read this first: limits of the data

- **35 rows: 10 South Africa, 10 UK, 10 US, 5 Remote.** 31 are ads I read fresh this round. 4 are consumer ads carried over from round one and not re-read (marked R1): Goodwall, Supernova Brands, Learntastic, and the ForceBrands natural food brand. I opened 33 ad pages this round against the limit of 40. Two were read and excluded (see the end of section A).
- **South Africa is thin, and 8 of the 10 SA rows are adjacent titles (adj).** "head of ecommerce" OR "ecommerce director" OR "head of e-commerce" in South Africa, past month, returned 0 results. Head of retention, head of CRM, head of performance marketing and marketplace head titles returned nothing at an SA consumer brand (9 results in total, 4 of them one agency's duplicate ad). I got to 10 rows, not the 12 you aimed for. Treat every SA percentage below as an indication, not a finding.
- **The US / Remote split is soft.** 7 of the 10 "US" rows are remote inside the US. I put a role under Remote when it was fully remote and either open across borders (Luxe, Fabula, Puffy, Airalo) or needed to fill the Remote group (the Kalon client, which is US remote). If you prefer, read US and Remote as one group of 15.
- **The profit field was only captured properly for the 31 fresh ads.** Round one did not ask for it. For the 4 R1 rows I report what my round-one notes hold and exclude them from the profit counts.
- **6 rows are recruiter-fronted** (ForceBrands x3, Mode Executive, ScaleJet, Kalon), and 4 of those do not name the brand. Recruiter copy is more generic than a brand's own words.
- **2 ads sit at the edge of the one-month window** (Frasers Group, Fabula).
- **Categories are my judgement.** Most ads never state a reason for hiring. Where the trigger is my reading of the responsibilities and not the ad's own statement, the row says "none stated".
- **A job ad is a biased lens for your question.** A brand that wants a fractional leader usually asks its network or a recruiter and never posts an ad. Low fractional counts here do not prove low fractional demand.
- No LinkedIn security check, captcha or warning appeared. Nothing was applied to, saved, followed or messaged. I worked in my own tab and closed it; your other tabs were not touched.

Key to role type: P = permanent, F = fractional, C = fixed-term contract or cover.
Key to profit field: YES = names margin, contribution margin, unit economics or P&L. PARTIAL = only the word "profitable", or P&L only as a candidate requirement. GROWTH ONLY = revenue, growth, traffic, ROI or ROAS only.

## G. Surprises, and categories I think are missing

Surprises:
1. **No fractional CMO ad for a consumer brand in South Africa or the UK.** All 3 fractional rows are US, and 2 of the 3 come through one recruiter (ForceBrands) for retail-led food and consumer brands, not DTC brands. Rates stated: $150/hr and $210/hr, at about 15 to 20 hours a week.
2. **The UK substitute for fractional is cover.** Dyson (12-month fixed term), Gozney (8-month maternity cover), and in the search results Coty (7-month fixed term) and Ennismore (maternity cover). I also saw part-time head roles for consumer marketplaces via the recruiter Propel in a results sidebar, which I did not open.
3. **Profit language is more common than I expected**: 17 of 31 name margin, unit economics or P&L, and 4 more say "profitable". The ads that do it best are precise: after returns, after financing, per platform, per ASIN, per incentive.
4. **Nobody says "our ad costs went up".** Only 5 ads have paid acquisition as the main trigger, and they phrase it as a discipline, not a complaint: "maintaining CAC ceiling" (Fabula), "marginal return rather than platform-reported averages" (Puffy), "paid-dependent acquisition" (Learntastic). The retention ads carry the same point from the other side.
5. **"Operator, not consultant" is said outright by the buyers most like your target.** Fabula: "An operator, not a consultant". ForceBrands: "not a traditional strategy consultant". Learntastic: "a hands-on operator role, not a pure strategist seat". A fractional offer that reads as advice will lose to this.
6. **Bringing work in-house from agencies is a stated goal.** Luxe wants "the single in-house owner" of Amazon and to manage "any remaining agencies"; Earthling will "build more of the acquisition capability in house over time". At the same time almost every smaller brand lists agencies to manage and "hold accountable".
7. **A foreign DTC brand is hiring senior ecommerce talent in South Africa at "$44,000 USD annually"** (Wallabaga, reporting to the CEO, owning a brand's Shopify, Amazon and TikTok Shop performance). That is both a competitor for SA talent and a hint that overseas brands will buy SA-based ecommerce leadership remotely.
8. **Dis-Chem is advertising three marketing heads at once** (Ecosystem Marketing, Brand & Marketing Strategy, Marketing Innovation), which looks like a restructure. I only read the first.
9. **The Samsonite SA ad shows "Reposted 2 hours ago" on an old job number**, which suggests the seat has been open or re-advertised for a while. That is my inference from the ID, not something the ad states.
10. MER, new-customer CAC and Power BI appear in none of the 35 ads.

Categories I think are missing from the list of 14:
- **Bring it in-house from agencies** (Luxe, Earthling). Different from "manage the agencies".
- **Concentration risk: one product or one channel carries the business** (Luxe's hero product "below 70%"; Wallabaga and Avenir building their own site beside marketplaces; Learntastic's dependence on paid).
- **Operating rhythm and decision rules** (Central Garden & Pet: "Predictable operating mechanisms that reduce surprises"; Puffy: "the rules that govern spend"). The hire is there to make performance run without the CEO.
- **Stock, cash and working capital on marketplaces** (F&F, Comfrt, Central). It sits half inside category 7 but is its own job.
- **Brand protection on marketplaces** (Luxe's unauthorised sellers; Central's "brand protection guardrails").
- **Sell more to the customers we already have through new services** (Dis-Chem), which is neither retention nor acquisition.
