# Why companies hire growth leadership: LinkedIn job ad research

Date read: 9 October 2026. Source: LinkedIn Jobs, Date posted = Past month. Read-only.
Ads read: 32 (South Africa 13, United Kingdom 11, Remote 8). No LinkedIn warnings or security checks appeared.

## Read this first (limits of the data)

- Small sample. 32 ads is enough to see patterns, not enough to trust percentages.
- South Africa returned almost nothing for the exact search terms: "fractional CMO" 0, "fractional CRO" 0, "entrepreneur in residence" 0, "chief revenue officer" 2, "head of growth" 7 (3 were duplicates of one ad), "revenue operations" 6 (all below head-of level). To reach 13 SA ads I added adjacent senior titles (CMO, CCO, Head of Sales, Sales and Marketing Director, Growth Director) found through a "go-to-market" keyword search at Director/Executive level and a "chief marketing officer" OR "chief commercial officer" search. Those rows are marked (adj).
- LinkedIn's quoted search for "go-to-market" returned zero in SA, which is a search quirk, not a real zero.
- 10 of the 32 ads were posted by recruiters or intermediaries for unnamed clients. Marked (via ...).
- Categories are my judgement. Several SA ads state no reason for hiring at all; I classified those from the role content and said so.
- One ad (#30) was about 4 weeks old, at the edge of the window.

Counts (32 ads):

- AI: requirement, nice-to-have or the job? Of the 9 AI ads, 7 treat it as a requirement (#1, 6, 7, 13, 16, 19, 25) and 2 as a nice-to-have (#11, 28). In 5 it is also written into the duties (#6, 7, 13, 16, 25). It was never the main reason for the hire.
- Automation named explicitly: 6 ads (#1, 6, 7, 19, 25, 28).
- The word "dashboard": only 3 ads (#6 "a live dashboard as the single source of truth", #8 "BI dashboards", #25 "performance dashboards"). The far more common wording is about trust in the numbers: "a forecast the board can trust" (#23), "a reliable view" (#22), "a forecast that's trusted and accurate" (#25), "whether our reporting is honest" (#4), "not simply activity or engagement" (#20).
- Building reporting or the revenue system is part of the job in 10 ads (#3, 6, 11, 20, 21, 22, 23, 25, 29, 31).
- Named tools appear in only 6 ads. HubSpot 3 (#1, 21, 29). Salesforce 2 (#14, 25). Google Analytics/GA4 2, Google Ads 2, Search Console 2 (#4, 29). Once each: Clay, Apollo, Lemlist, SAP, Groove, Outreach, CallRail, Microsoft Ads. SQL in 2 as a nice-to-have. No ad named Looker or Power BI.
- Does SA differ? On this sample SA asks for AI at least as often as the UK (4 of 13 against 3 of 11), and frames it as a leadership mandate rather than a tool skill: "Lead the adoption of AI across marketing" (#7 and #13, near identical wording) and "designing and running agents that scale the sales motion" (#6). UK and Remote ads are more specific about the stack (HubSpot, Salesforce, Clay) and about revenue operations as a discipline. SA had no senior revenue operations role at all, against 15 at director level in the UK. SA also had the most ads with no tooling mention (5 of 13). Treat all of this as directional: the samples are small.

## E. Surprises, and categories I think are missing

Surprises:

1. "Fractional" is barely advertised. SA: zero. UK: one result for "fractional CMO" OR "fractional CRO", plus the Practicus Fractional CRO. Worldwide remote: 9 results, nearly all US. The need exists (see the permanent ads) but buyers do not post it as a fractional job. You will not find SA demand by searching for the word.
2. When fractional roles are advertised, an intermediary usually posts them: 4 of the 6 true fractional ads (Fractional Leaders Group, Practicus, BioX, ForceBrands). These networks are both a channel and a competitor.
3. The market is openly hostile to "consultant". "Doers, not consultants." (#16) "This isn't high-altitude consulting." (#21) "a hands-on operator role, not a pure strategist seat" (#28) "a builder, not a caretaker" (#7) "hands-on builder rather than a hands-off executive" (#11). Only one ad (#27) wanted diagnosis without execution.
4. Nobody says someone left. Category 5 as "a departure" appeared in zero ads. It shows up only as "nobody owns this" (#4) or as fractional acting as a bridge to a permanent hire (#21, 30, 31).
5. Founder-led ceiling was a UK finding. It was the stated main trigger in 2 UK ads and 0 SA ads. SA triggers skewed to expansion into the rest of Africa (4 of 13) and to large corporate seats with no stated reason.
6. The pain is trust in the numbers, not a lack of dashboards. See section D.
7. One Cape Town fintech is hiring a CMO, COO, CBO and CTO at the same time (#13). That is the clearest interim-leadership gap in the SA set, but the company is unnamed.
8. "Head of Growth" means very different jobs: enterprise sales (#12, 17), franchise finance (#8), product (#2), outbound operations (#1).

Categories I think are missing:

- First hire in the seat / build the function from scratch (#3, 5, 9, 19, 20, 23). Different from founder-led ceiling: no founder is selling, the function simply does not exist yet.
- Prove the return on marketing spend and hold agencies accountable (#4, 24, 29, 31). I put these under category 2 but it is a distinct buyer problem, and it is where your dashboards fit best.
